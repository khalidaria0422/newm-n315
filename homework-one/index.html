Start Producing Music

There are 4 essential tools needed to start producing music. Learn about each one below.

The 4 Essential Tools

Digital Audio Workstation (DAW)

A DAW is software used to record, edit, arrange, and produce music on a computer. Different DAWs offer many of the same core features, but each may be better suited for certain workflows. For example, some focus more on recording and mixing, while others are popular for electronic music, beat making, or live performance.

Audio Interface

An audio interface connects microphones, instruments, headphones, and speakers to your computer and converts audio between analog and digital signals. Interfaces can differ in sound quality, number of inputs and outputs, connection type, and available features. A simple interface may have only two inputs, while larger models can handle many instruments and microphones at once.

Microphone

A microphone captures vocals, instruments, and other sounds so they can be recorded. Two common types are dynamic and condenser microphones. Dynamic microphones are generally durable and work well with loud sound sources, while condenser microphones are typically more sensitive and can capture greater detail, making them common for studio vocals and acoustic instruments.

Headphones & Speakers

Headphones and studio speakers, often called studio monitors, allow you to hear and evaluate the music you are producing. Headphones are useful for detailed listening, recording, and working without disturbing others, while monitors let you hear how a mix sounds in a room. Different models can vary in frequency response, accuracy, comfort, size, and how much bass or detail they reproduce.
